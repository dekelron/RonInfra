# ImageNet-trained CNNs are biased towards texture; increasing shape bias improves accuracy and robustness

- **Authors:** Robert Geirhos, Patricia Rubisch, Claudio Michaelis, Matthias
  Bethge, Felix Wichmann, Wieland Brendel
- **Venue / Year:** ICLR 2019 (Oral)
- **ID:** arXiv:1811.12231
- **Cluster:** D — human/DNN perception foundations
- **Sourcing:** `verified`

## What it does

Uses **texture–shape cue-conflict** stimuli (e.g., cat shape with elephant
texture) and compares CNNs vs. human observers in a controlled psychophysical
paradigm. Introduces Stylized-ImageNet to induce shape bias.

## Key findings

- ImageNet-trained CNNs are **texture-biased**; humans are **shape-biased** — a
  fundamentally different classification strategy.
- Training toward shape bias improves accuracy and **robustness**.

## Relevance to our artifact

A canonical example of **controlled-stimulus, human-vs-model psychophysics**
revealing a strategy difference invisible to accuracy benchmarks — the spirit of
our project. Also a positioning-rule citation (credit Geirhos et al.).

## How we cite / differentiate

Cite as foundational human-vs-DNN comparison methodology. Differentiate: cue-
conflict is **categorical** (which cue wins); ours is a **parametric transducer**
(threshold + slope) with a **causal** manipulation.
