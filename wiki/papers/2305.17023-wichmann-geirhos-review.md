# Are Deep Neural Networks Adequate Behavioral Models of Human Visual Perception?

- **Authors:** Felix A. Wichmann (Tübingen), Robert Geirhos (Google Research)
- **Venue / Year:** *Annual Review of Vision Science*, Vol. 9, Sep 2023;
  arXiv 2023
- **ID:** arXiv:2305.17023
- **Cluster:** C — methodological sibling / framing anchor
- **Sourcing:** `verified` (review); details `snippet`

## What it does

A review arguing DNNs are **valuable scientific tools but not yet adequate
computational models** of human core object recognition. Stresses distinguishing
**statistical tools** from **computational models**, and treating model quality
as **multidimensional** with explicit modeling goals.

## Key findings / stance

- Promising but not adequate; be precise about what "model of perception" claims.
- Warns against over-reading behavioural similarity as mechanistic equivalence.

## Relevance to our artifact

The **framing anchor** and a red-team lens. Keeps us honest: we report human
alignment as *comparison*, and our positive claim is about **method + causal
mechanism**, not "VLMs perceive like humans."

## How we cite / differentiate

Cite in related work as the framing we adopt (tool vs. model; goal clarity), and
in the discussion to pre-empt "you're overclaiming human-likeness." Our
mechanism-disambiguation (log-encoding vs scalar-noise) is precisely the kind of
goal-explicit, multidimensional test the review calls for.
