# Testing the Limits of Fine-Tuning for Improving Visual Cognition in Vision Language Models

## What it does

Fine-tunes VLMs (incl. LoRA-style parameter-efficient adaptation, frozen
backbone) on **intuitive-physics / causal-reasoning** visual-cognition tasks and
tests whether alignment with human judgments improves and generalizes.

## Key findings

- Fine-tuning **improves** performance and human-alignment on the trained
  cognitive domains…
- …but the improvement is **brittle** — physical/causal reasoning does not
  generalize robustly to new situations.

## Relevance to our artifact

This is the closest neighbor to our **causal LoRA** component. It shows
"fine-tuning changes visual behaviour" is already known — so the LoRA cannot be
pitched as novel *in itself*.

## How we cite / differentiate

Our claim is **sharper and lower-level**: we move a **parametric psychophysical
threshold / transducer slope**, a continuous, pre-registered quantity — not
high-level cognition. Their **brittleness** finding is an *argument for* our
approach: measuring at the psychophysical level gives a controlled, falsifiable
target where "did it move, and by how much" is unambiguous. Pre-register the
effect-size bar (see 05-open-questions).
