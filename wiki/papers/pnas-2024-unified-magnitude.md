# A unified framework for perceived magnitude and discriminability of sensory stimuli

- **Authors:** (PNAS 2024; confirm in PDF)
- **Venue / Year:** *PNAS* 2024
- **ID:** DOI 10.1073/pnas.2312293121
- **Cluster:** I — theory of the log response
- **Sourcing:** `snippet`

## What it does

Reconciles **Weber's law**, **Fechner's logarithmic law**, and **Stevens' power
law** in one framework: **perceived magnitude** and **discriminability** access
different aspects of a **single underlying representation**; under stated
conditions Weber and Stevens **coexist** consistently.

## Key findings

- Discrimination-based (Fechner/Weber) and magnitude-estimation-based (Stevens)
  measures need not conflict — they measure different functionals of the same
  transducer + noise.

## Relevance to our artifact

Directly governs **how we fit and interpret** the transducer. It warns that
"log vs. power law" depends on **which measurement** (discrimination vs. magnitude
estimation) — so our choice of behavioural paradigm (2AFC discrimination vs.
matching/estimation) determines which law we should even expect. Feeds open
question #1.

## How we cite / differentiate

Cite as the theoretical basis for our statistics/interpretation and for choosing
the discrimination paradigm. It's a framework we *apply*, not a competitor.
