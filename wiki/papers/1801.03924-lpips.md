# The Unreasonable Effectiveness of Deep Features as a Perceptual Metric (LPIPS)

- **Authors:** Richard Zhang, Phillip Isola, Alexei A. Efros, Eli Shechtman,
  Oliver Wang
- **Venue / Year:** CVPR 2018
- **ID:** arXiv:1801.03924
- **Cluster:** D — human/DNN perception foundations
- **Sourcing:** `verified`

## What it does

Shows deep-network features (VGG/AlexNet, various training) predict **human
perceptual similarity judgments** far better than classic metrics (SSIM, PSNR).
Introduces **LPIPS** and the BAPPS human-judgment dataset (2AFC / JND).

## Key findings

- Deep features are a strong **perceptual-loss / similarity** metric across
  architectures and training regimes — "unreasonably effective."

## Relevance to our artifact

Directly connected to Dekel-2017's "DNN features as estimators of **perceptual
loss**." LPIPS is the widely-adopted embodiment of that idea and a required
related-work citation. Its 2AFC/JND human-data methodology is a reference for our
behavioural discrimination protocol.

## How we cite / differentiate

Cite as the mainstream perceptual-loss metric and as methodological reference for
2AFC/JND. Differentiate: LPIPS is a **static similarity metric** fit to human
judgments; ours **measures a transducer law inside VLMs** and moves it causally.
