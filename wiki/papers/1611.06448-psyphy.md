# PsyPhy: A Psychophysics Driven Evaluation Framework for Visual Recognition

- **Authors:** Brandon RichardWebster, Samuel E. Anthony, Walter J. Scheirer
- **Venue / Year:** arXiv 2016; TPAMI 2019
- **ID:** arXiv:1611.06448
- **Cluster:** C — methodological sibling (named in the kickoff)
- **Sourcing:** `verified` (well-known); details `snippet`

## What it does

Introduces psychophysics as an **evaluation methodology** for visual-recognition
models: rather than summary accuracy, it uses **item-response curves** over
**procedurally rendered** stimuli (millions of 3D scenes / 2D images) with
controlled perturbations to recover **perceptual thresholds** for CNNs, and
compares them to human item-response behaviour.

## Key findings

- CNNs that look humanlike on aggregate accuracy diverge from humans on
  item-response curves under controlled perturbations — questioning "humanlike
  performance" claims.

## Relevance to our artifact

The **canonical precedent** for "psychophysics as model evaluation," and the
direct answer to interview Q2 (**scaling to millions**): PsyPhy already scaled
psychophysics to millions of *procedurally generated* stimuli. We inherit that
logic — procedural stimuli + adaptive sampling → volume is a compute dial.

target a **specific transducer law** (contrast/Weber) in **VLMs**, behaviourally
