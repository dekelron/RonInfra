## What it does

Estimates the **contrast sensitivity function (CSF)** of chat-based MLLMs
behaviourally. Presents bandpass-filtered noise at varying spatial frequency and
contrast; collects **yes/no** verbal responses; builds **psychometric functions**
(fit with a **Weibull** CDF); extracts **contrast *detection* thresholds**;
computes CSF = 1 / threshold. Models: GPT, Gemini (API). Prompt variants analyzed.

## Key findings

- Some models resemble human CSFs in **shape or scale, but none capture both**.
- CSF estimates are **highly sensitive to prompt phrasing** (limited linguistic
  robustness).
- CSFs **predict** model behaviour under frequency-filtered / adversarial inputs.

## Relevance to our artifact

The closest behavioural-psychophysics-on-VLMs work. If we pitched "behavioural
contrast psychophysics on VLMs" without qualification, this is the reviewer's
"already done." Their **Weibull-fit + yes/no staircase** is a concrete method
reference for our own protocol; their **prompt-sensitivity** finding is a
mandatory robustness control for us.

## How we cite / differentiate

**Detection ≠ suprathreshold transducer.** They measure the *lowest perceivable*
contrast (near-threshold, per spatial frequency). We measure the **suprathreshold
transducer shape** (Weber-of-discrimination / contrast constancy), in **behaviour
*and* the vision encoder**, and we **causally manipulate** it (LoRA). Different
regime, extra surface, extra stance. Cite prominently; distinguish in the abstract.
