## What it does

Asks whether human system-level visual perception has DNN correlates. Maps
several human perceptual sensitivities onto layer-wise computation in
ImageNet-trained models (VGG-19, CaffeNet, GoogLeNet, ResNet), treating DNN
features as **estimators of perceptual loss**. Measurement is **representational**
(feature distances / layer activations), not behavioural.

## Key findings

- Sensitivity to **image changes** → *mid*-computation correlates.
- **Segmentation, crowding, shape** → *end*-computation correlates.
- ⭐ **The log-response result:** logarithmically-spaced contrast inputs become
  **linearly spaced** at end-computation layers; average change in DNN
  representation scales with the **log** of input-change magnitude; output L1 vs.
  log-contrast fit at **R² ≈ 98%** (avg across spatial frequencies). A
  Weber–Fechner-like transducer emerges without being trained for.
- Framed as consistent with human perceptual properties arising from
  architecture-independent visual learning.
