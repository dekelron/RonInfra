## What it does

Asks whether human system-level visual perception has DNN correlates. Maps
several human perceptual sensitivities onto layer-wise computation in
ImageNet-trained models (VGG-19, CaffeNet, GoogLeNet, ResNet), treating DNN
features as **estimators of perceptual loss**. Measurement is **representational**
(feature distances / layer activations), not behavioural.

## Key findings

- Sensitivity to **image changes** → *mid*-computation correlates.
- **Segmentation, crowding, shape** → *end*-computation correlates.
- ⭐ **The log-response result (R²≈98% confirmed via search):** stimulus = a gray
  reference image vs. **sinusoidal gratings** across spatial-frequency × contrast;
  measure **L1** = mean absolute change in DNN representation. Response is
  **bandpass** at low contrast (modulated by spatial frequency, like the human
  CSF) but **~constant at high contrast** at end-computation layers (**contrast
  constancy**). Log-spaced contrast inputs become **linearly spaced** at end
  layers; output (prob) L1 vs. **log-contrast** fits at **R² ≈ 98%** (avg across
  spatial frequencies). A Weber–Fechner-like transducer emerges without being
  trained for.
- Framed as consistent with human perceptual properties arising from
  architecture-independent visual learning.
