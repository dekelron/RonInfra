# Crowding Reveals Fundamental Differences in Local vs. Global Processing in Humans and Machines

- **Authors:** Adrien Doerig, Alban Bornet, Oh-Hyeon Choung, Michael H. Herzog
- **Venue / Year:** *Vision Research* 2020 (bioRxiv 2019)
- **ID:** arXiv:2004.12676
- **Cluster:** E — crowding (prior art for the demoted add-on)
- **Sourcing:** `snippet`

## What it does

Uses **crowding / uncrowding** as a probe of global shape computation. Tests
whether feedforward CNNs reproduce human (un)crowding, where adding **more**
flankers can *reduce* crowding ("uncrowding") — a global-configuration effect.

## Key findings

- Feedforward CNNs reproduce basic **crowding** (pooling target + flankers along
  the hierarchy) but **cannot produce uncrowding** for principled architectural
  reasons.
- Argues **recurrent global grouping/segmentation** is required.

## Relevance to our artifact

Defines the **prior art** that scopes our crowding add-on. Crowding in nets is
already studied **representationally**; the **open** slice is *behavioural*
crowding + **uncrowding** in **chat VLMs**, on synthetic + real-UI stimuli.
Uncrowding is the strongest differentiator (feedforward nets fail it).
